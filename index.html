About

I am a quantitative scientist with expertise in applied mathematics, statistics, operations research and computational modelling. My work focuses on developing practical quantitative methods to support decision-making when uncertainty is high, data are limited, and resources must be allocated efficiently.

My research and applied work span biosecurity, ecology, fisheries, risk analysis, optimisation, surveillance design, Bayesian inference, resource allocation and decision support systems. I enjoy translating complex real-world problems into mathematical and statistical frameworks that provide clear and actionable insights.

Over the course of my career, I have worked across theoretical computer science, algorithm design, operations research, environmental modelling, fisheries science and biosecurity. My goal is to bridge rigorous quantitative methods with practical decision-making.

Current Research Themes

Emerging Biosecurity Threats

Developing quantitative methods that support preparedness, surveillance, eradication and proof-of-freedom programmes for invasive pests and diseases. This includes statistical methods for detection, surveillance design, resource prioritisation and decision-making under uncertainty.

Fisheries and Marine Resource Management

Applying statistical modelling, risk analysis and optimisation to fisheries monitoring, bycatch estimation, electronic monitoring systems and sustainable resource management.

Resource Allocation under Uncertainty

Developing methods to help decision-makers allocate limited resources efficiently across competing interventions, balancing cost, risk and operational objectives.

Decision Science

Building analytical and computational tools that support evidence-based decision-making in complex systems. My work combines Bayesian methods, optimisation, simulation and statistical modelling.
